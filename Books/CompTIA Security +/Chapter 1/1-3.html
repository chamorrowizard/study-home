📌 Chapter Overview

Operational Security

Uncontrolled changes create security vulnerabilities and service outages. Formal change management ensures every system modification is vetted through business approvals, tested for technical dependencies, scheduled to minimize downtime, and backed by robust documentation.

Governance & Impact

CAB approvals, stakeholder buy-in, owner accountability, and risk impact analysis before execution.

Technical Safeguards

Controlled allow/deny rules, maintenance windows, rollback plans, and versioned SOPs.

1. Business Approvals & Change Advisory Board (CAB)

Objective 1.3.1

Why & How: Changes must not occur in isolation. The Change Advisory Board (CAB) reviews proposed system modifications to evaluate security risk, business continuity impact, and resource requirements before granting formal authorization.

~/change_mgmt/cab_approval.sh
cab-review --ticket RFC-9021 --reviewers security,ops,legal --approve
$

2. Ownership, Stakeholders & Impact Analysis

Objective 1.3.2

Why & How: Every Request for Change (RFC) requires a designated System Owner who assumes accountability. An Impact Analysis identifies affected stakeholders, potential security risks, and business disruption before execution.

~/change_mgmt/impact_analysis.py
analyze-impact --owner sysadmin_sec --scope production --risk-score calculated
$

3. Allow Lists & Deny Lists Controls

Objective 1.3.3

Why & How: Technical change enforcement relies on explicit policy controls. Allow Listing restricts execution/traffic strictly to approved entities (Zero Trust default), while Deny Listing blocks known malicious or unauthorized assets.

~/change_mgmt/access_policy.config
policy-config --mode allow-list --approved-hash signed_app.sha256 --block-untrusted
$

4. Maintenance Windows & Dependency Mapping

Objective 1.3.4

Why & How: High-risk modifications are restricted to pre-scheduled Maintenance Windows to minimize user impact during expected downtime. Teams must map technical Dependencies to avoid cascading infrastructure failures.

~/change_mgmt/schedule_window.yaml
schedule-change --window sunday_0200_utc --map-deps database,auth_service --downtime 30m
$

5. Documentation, SOPs & Rollback Plans

Objective 1.3.5

Why & How: Execution requires step-by-step Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) tracked via Version Control (e.g., Git). Every change plan MUST include an approved Backout/Rollback Plan to quickly revert system state if errors occur.

~/change_mgmt/version_sop.sh
sop-deploy --version v2.4.1 --backout-script rollback_v2.4.0.sh --log-audit true
$

6. Staging & Sandbox Validation

Objective 1.3.6

Why & How: Changes must never be deployed directly to production without testing. Changes are first deployed in an isolated Staging/Sandbox Environment to validate stability, run security regression testing, and verify that dependencies react as expected.

~/change_mgmt/staging_test.sh
validate-change --env staging --run-regression --verify-zero-downtime
$

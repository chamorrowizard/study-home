📌 Chapter Overview

Core Architecture

Security architecture relies on balanced protections across core principles, access control frameworks, modern perimeter-less environments, and physical safeguards. Master these foundation pillars to design resilient infrastructure.

The Core Pillars

CIA Triad protects information assets; Non-repudiation ensures accountability via cryptographic attribution.

Access & Boundaries

AAA regulates identities, while Zero Trust enforces continuous verification across data and control planes.

1. Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (CIA)

Objective 1.2.1

Why & How: The CIA Triad is the bedrock security model. Confidentiality prevents unauthorized data exposure (using AES encryption). Integrity guarantees data remains unaltered (using cryptographic SHA-256 hashes). Availability ensures services remain accessible when needed (using redundant clusters and load balancers).

~/security/cia_triad.sh
encrypt --mode AES-256 --hash SHA-256 --HA-cluster true
$

2. Non-repudiation

Objective 1.2.2

Why & How: Non-repudiation ensures that a sender or actor cannot deny the authenticity of their signature, message, or action. It is achieved by combining digital signatures (Asymmetric Encryption + Hashing) with strict proof of origin and timing via Trusted Timestamping Authorities (TSA).

~/security/non_repudiation.py
sign_payload(msg, private_key) --timestamp-authority rfc3161
$

3. Authentication, Authorization, and Accounting (AAA)

Objective 1.2.3

Why & How: AAA regulates user access life cycles. Authentication verifies who you are (credentials/MFA). Authorization dictates what you can access (RBAC policies). Accounting logs what you actually did (audit trails, session metrics via RADIUS/TACACS+).

~/security/aaa_policy.json
aaa-service --auth MFA --grant RBAC-Admin --log-audit syslog
$

4. Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA)

Objective 1.2.4

Why & How: ZTA operates on "Never Trust, Always Verify". The Control Plane manages security policies: the Policy Engine (PE) decides access, while the Policy Administrator (PA) issues dynamic access credentials. The Data Plane handles actual traffic passing through the Policy Enforcement Point (PEP).

~/security/zero_trust.config
zta-control-plane --pe-eval trust_score --pa-issue-token --pep-enforce
$

5. Physical Security Controls

Objective 1.2.5

Why & How: Digital defenses fail if physical assets are compromised. Physical controls use layered defense-in-depth: Bollards stop vehicle attacks, Access Vestibules (Mantraps) prevent tailgating, Guards & Sensors detect intrusions, and Video Surveillance (CCTV) provides recordable oversight.

~/security/physical_sec.yaml
deploy-physical --bollards K12 --vestibule interlocking --cctv motion_sense
$

6. Deception & Disruption Technologies

Objective 1.2.6

Why & How: Deception controls trap, delay, and trace attackers by deploying decoy assets. Honeypots and Honeynets simulate vulnerable servers or entire networks, while Honeytokens and Honeyfiles act as traceably monitored fake credentials or data files that trigger immediate alerts upon interaction.

~/security/deception_trap.sh
deploy-trap --honeypot interactive --honeytoken canary_db_pass --alert immediate
$

7. Gap Analysis

Objective 1.2.7

Why & How: Gap analysis evaluates the distance between an organization's current baseline security posture and its target framework requirements (e.g., NIST, ISO 27001). It identifies missing technical controls, operational deficiencies, and compliance weaknesses to guide resource allocation.

~/security/gap_analysis.py
audit-posture --current baseline_v1 --target NIST-CSF-2.0 --report gaps
$

8. Authentication & Authorization Models

Objective 1.2.8

Why & How: AAA differentiates between Authenticating People (MFA, biometric, passwords) and Authenticating Systems (API keys, mTLS, service accounts). Once identity is proven, access is managed through Authorization Models like RBAC (Role-Based), ABAC (Attribute-Based), or DAC/MAC.

~/security/access_models.sh
auth-system --type mTLS --service-account --model ABAC --attr department=IT
$

9. Perimeter Controls & Detection Sensors

Objective 1.2.9

Why & How: Complete physical security extends beyond entryways. Fencing, Lighting, & Access Badges establish perimeter boundaries, while environmental & intrusion Sensors (Infrared, Pressure, Microwave, and Ultrasonic) detect movement or physical breaches across restricted areas.

~/security/perimeter_sensors.config
sensor-config --type infrared --motion ultrasonic --perimeter fencing_zone1
$
